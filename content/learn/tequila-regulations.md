Tequila is a *Denomination of Origin* product. Like Champagne or Cognac, the name is legally protected, and only spirit made under specific conditions can use it.

To be sold as tequila, the spirit must:

- **Come from the denomination of origin:** all of Jalisco, plus designated municipalities in Nayarit, Tamaulipas, Michoacán and Guanajuato.
- **Be made from blue Weber agave** (*Agave tequilana Weber, variedad azul*). Plain "tequila" — known as *mixto* — needs at least 51% of its sugars from agave; the rest can come from other sugars. Bottles labeled *100% de agave* must use blue agave only.
- **Be produced under the [Consejo Regulador del Tequila (CRT)](https://www.crt.org.mx/)**, which certifies and audits every distillery and assigns each one a [NOM number](/learn/nom-numbers).
- **Be bottled in Mexico if it's 100% de agave.** Mixto can be shipped in bulk and bottled abroad.
- **Be between 35% and 55% alcohol** by volume.

What the rules don't cover is much of what enthusiasts care about. [Additives](/learn/additive-free-tequila) are allowed up to 1% without disclosure. [Diffusers](/learn/diffuser), autoclaves and column stills are all permitted. That gap between the legal minimum and traditional practice is exactly where programs like additive-free verification — and sites like this one — come in.
