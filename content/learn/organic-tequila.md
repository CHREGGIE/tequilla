An organic tequila has been certified by a third-party body — for the US market, usually to USDA Organic standards — that checks how the agave was farmed and how the tequila was made. Broadly, that means agave grown without synthetic pesticides, herbicides or fertilizers, and a production process that keeps certified-organic inputs separate from conventional ones.

Agave takes six to eight years or more to mature, so organic certification is a long commitment: a field has to be managed organically for years before it can be harvested as organic. That's part of why genuinely organic tequila costs more.

The thing to keep straight is that **organic and [additive-free](/learn/additive-free-tequila) are different claims.** Organic is about the farming and the process. Additive-free is about what goes into the bottle at the end. A tequila can be organic and still use permitted additives, and plenty of excellent additive-free tequilas aren't certified organic. If you care about both, check for both.
