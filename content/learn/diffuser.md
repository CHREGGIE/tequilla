A *difusor* — diffuser — is industrial equipment borrowed from sugar refining. Instead of cooking whole agave piñas for a day or more and then crushing them, the distillery shreds the raw agave and runs it through a long chamber where hot water and pressure (and sometimes acid) wash the sugars out.

It's extremely efficient: it pulls more sugar from each plant, takes hours instead of days, and needs far less labor. The trade-off is flavor. The slow cooking that builds caramelized, roasted-agave character barely happens, and the resulting spirit is often described as thin, flat or faintly medicinal.

That's why diffusers are so closely tied to the [additive](/learn/additive-free-tequila) debate: a little glycerin, sweetener and oak extract can cover for what the process didn't build. Tequila made with a diffuser is legal and doesn't have to say so on the label. Some of the best-known celebrity and mass-market brands are made this way; very few additive-free tequilas are.
