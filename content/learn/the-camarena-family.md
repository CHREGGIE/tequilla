The Camarenas are one of tequila's great production families, and two of their distilleries are touchstones for anyone who cares about traditional, additive-free tequila. Both sit in the Jalisco highlands, around Arandas.

## La Alteña — NOM 1139

Don Felipe Camarena founded La Alteña in 1937, near Arandas, on land where he already grew agave. Its original brand is **Tapatio**. In the late 1980s the family partnered with American importers to create **El Tesoro de Don Felipe**, which helped kick off the premium tequila boom in the US. The El Tesoro brand is now owned by Suntory and made at La Alteña under contract; Tapatio remains the family's own.

Production is famously old-school: brick ovens, a [tahona](/learn/tahona) that is still in regular use, wood fermentation and copper pot stills. Carlos Camarena, the founder's grandson, led the distillery for decades and became one of the best-known advocates for traditional methods; the next generation of the family is now part of running it.

## El Pandillo — NOM 1579

Felipe J. Camarena — Carlos's brother, and also a grandson of the founder — set out on his own in 2011 and built Destilería El Pandillo in Jesús María, a highland town southeast of Arandas. An engineer by temperament, he designed much of the equipment himself, and the distillery collects rainwater for production. El Pandillo makes **G4**, named for the generations of Camarena tequila makers, and **Pasote**, among others.

If you're drinking Tapatio, El Tesoro, G4 or Pasote, you're drinking the work of one family's long commitment to making tequila the slow way.
