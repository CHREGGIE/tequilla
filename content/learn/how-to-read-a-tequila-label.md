A tequila label is part legal disclosure, part marketing. Learn to read the first part and you can mostly ignore the second.

1. **"100% de agave" — or not.** If a bottle just says *Tequila*, it's a mixto: as little as 51% agave sugars, and it may have been bottled outside Mexico. If it's 100% agave, the label will say so, prominently. This is the first thing to check.

2. **The category.** Blanco (or plata), joven, reposado, añejo, extra añejo. That tells you how long it spent in wood — see [the types of tequila](/learn/types-of-tequila). *Cristalino* is a style, usually an añejo filtered clear.

3. **The NOM.** Usually small, on the back: *NOM 1146*, for example. It tells you which distillery actually made the tequila, whatever the brand name says. See [what a NOM number is](/learn/nom-numbers).

4. **Alcohol content.** Tequila must be 35–55% alcohol. Most bottles are 38–40%. Higher-proof bottlings (*high proof*, *still strength*) often show the distillery's character more clearly.

5. **Production details — when they're there.** Nothing obliges a producer to say how the tequila was made, so the ones who do usually want you to know: *horno de mampostería* (brick oven), *tahona*, *fermentación natural*, *alambique de cobre* (copper pot still), the agave's region or estate. A label that says nothing about production isn't proof of shortcuts — but a label that's all celebrity and no process is a hint.

What the label **won't** tell you: whether the tequila contains [additives](/learn/additive-free-tequila), or whether it was made with a [diffuser](/learn/diffuser). For those, you need independent sources.
