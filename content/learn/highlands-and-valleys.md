Most tequila comes from two parts of Jalisco, and you'll see them everywhere on labels and in tasting notes.

**Los Altos — the highlands.** East of Guadalajara, around Arandas, Jesús María and Atotonilco el Alto. Higher altitude, cooler nights and red, iron-rich clay soil. Highland agave tends to grow larger and sweeter, and highland tequila is usually described as fruitier, more floral and softer. La Alteña and El Pandillo are both here.

**The valley — Valles, often called the lowlands.** West of Guadalajara, around the towns of Tequila, Amatitán and El Arenal, under the Volcán de Tequila. Dark volcanic soil. Valley tequila is usually described as earthier and more herbal, with pepper and minerality. La Fortaleza and many of the oldest distilleries are here.

Treat this as a tendency, not a law. How the agave is cooked, crushed, fermented and distilled changes a tequila far more than where it grew, and many brands blend agave from both regions. But if you taste a highland and a valley blanco side by side, you'll usually tell them apart.
