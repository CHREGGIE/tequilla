Mexican rules allow tequila — even bottles labeled *100% de agave* — to contain up to 1% by weight of four kinds of additives, known as *abocantes*: glycerin (for a rounder mouthfeel), syrup-based sweeteners, caramel coloring, and oak extract. None of it has to appear on the label.

One percent sounds small. It isn't. A little glycerin and sugar can smooth over harsh, fast production, and caramel and oak extract can make a few weeks in a barrel look and taste like years.

Additive-free tequila uses none of them. What you taste comes from the agave, how it was cooked and fermented, the still, and — for aged tequila — time in real wood.

Because producers aren't required to disclose additives, "additive-free" isn't something a brand can prove by saying it. The de facto standard is the independent verification program run by [Tequila Matchmaker](https://www.tequilamatchmaker.com/), which tests samples and visits distilleries. Tequillist's additive-free flags are meant to follow that verified list — if you spot a mismatch, tell us.
